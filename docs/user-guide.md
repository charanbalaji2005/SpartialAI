# User guide

## Taking photos that reconstruct well
Shoot in even light with the camera level. Keep the main subject fully in frame with some space around it. Avoid motion blur, mirrors, glass and very dark scenes. Corners of rooms and streets with clear perspective give the most convincing depth. For a single object, a three-quarter view shows more of its shape than a straight-on view.

## Making a scene
Drop a photo on the Scenes page or choose **Choose photo**. On the next screen pick a quality mode: Fast for a quick look, Balanced for most photos, Best quality for final results. Choose **Generate 3D scene**. The progress screen shows each step, GPU memory and the log; **Cancel** stops at the next step.

## Exploring
The scene opens from the photo's own viewpoint, so it first looks like the photo. Drag to orbit, right-drag or two-finger drag to pan, and scroll or pinch to zoom. Press `V` to return to the photo's viewpoint. Use `W`, `E` and `R` for the move, rotate and scale gizmos.

The shading buttons change how surfaces look. **Photo** shows the photo's colours unchanged. **Lit** adds the lighting estimated from the photo. **Clay** removes textures so you can judge shape. **Seen vs inferred** colours everything by where it came from: teal was visible in the photo, lilac was inferred, green was generated by the object model.

## Working with objects
Click an object in the viewport or the scene list to select it. The properties panel shows how confident the detector was, which method cut it out, its distance, and how much of it was seen versus inferred. Change position, rotation and scale there; changes are offsets from where it was reconstructed. Use the eye icons to hide parts, for example an inferred back you don't want.

## Exporting
In the bottom panel, **Export** offers the original scene as GLB or glTF, its metadata, and **Download edited scene**, which saves your moves and (optionally) only visible parts. **Assets** has individual objects, depth maps and textures. GLB files open in Blender (File > Import > glTF 2.0), Windows 3D Viewer, and most game engines.

## Reading the quality report
"Seen in photo" is the share of the scene's faces that came straight from the photo; lower means more guessing. "Edges cut at depth jumps" shows how much was removed to avoid stretched surfaces. "Fallbacks used" lists any model that wasn't available and what replaced it.
